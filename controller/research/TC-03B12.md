# TC-03B12 — clock tolerance and timestamp uncertainty

Status: portable timing/freshness evidence contract only. Target remains the 2027-28 Terps Racing EV torque-control project.

## Decision

A nominal timestamp frequency is not enough to justify a real-time threshold. After TC-03B11 derives the nominal DWT/timer/SysTick rate from the actual clock tree, TC-03B12 requires a source-specific minimum and maximum frequency bound over the intended operating conditions before converting tick counts into control/fault elapsed time.

The distinction is important on STM32F405. ST's product page summarizes the internal 16 MHz HSI as factory-trimmed to 1% accuracy, but DS8626 Rev 12 Table 34 is conditional: HSI accuracy is -1%/+1% at 25 °C under the table's factory-calibration condition, -4%/+4% from -10 to 85 °C, and -8%/+4.5% from -40 to 105 °C. Therefore the 1% summary must not be promoted into a 2027-28 vehicle clock guarantee without the actual oscillator source, board condition and reviewed operating envelope.

If the team uses HSE, the STM32 datasheet directs the designer to the resonator manufacturer for frequency/accuracy characteristics. The MCU documentation alone does not establish board-level HSE tolerance.

No Formula SAE rule threshold is derived in this pass.

## Threshold directions

For a counter with frequency in [f_min, f_max], a tick delta N corresponds to:
- earliest possible elapsed time: N / f_max;
- nominal elapsed time: N / f_nominal;
- latest possible elapsed time: N / f_min.

Two requirements therefore use opposite conservative conversions:

1. Minimum persistence: if a condition must exist for at least T seconds before acting, use ceil(f_max × T) ticks.
2. Maximum allowed age: if data must be declared stale no later than T seconds, use floor(f_min × T) ticks. This may trigger by less than one slow-clock tick early, which is conservative for a maximum-age requirement.

A nominal-only threshold does not provide both guarantees when clock rate is uncertain.

## Synthetic counterexample

The portable fixture uses a synthetic 168 MHz nominal clock with ±1% bounds only to exercise the arithmetic. It is not a Terps Racing clock claim and is deliberately not treated as an operating-range HSI guarantee.

For a nominal 100 ms threshold:
- nominal threshold: 16,800,000 ticks;
- at the +1% frequency bound, those ticks occur at about 99.0099 ms;
- at the -1% frequency bound, they occur at about 101.0101 ms;
- minimum-persistence threshold: 16,968,000 ticks;
- maximum-age threshold: 16,632,000 ticks.

For two initially aligned independent clocks each bounded to ±1% and interpreted at their own nominal rate, their reported elapsed times can separate by up to 20 ms over one real second. That is a rate-error envelope only; it does not establish phase alignment, common capture or synchronization.

## Source-specific worker packet

Prerequisites:
1. TC-03A identifies the actual board/firmware revision and clock-related source files.
2. TC-03B0 reproduces the untouched build.
3. TC-03B11 binds nominal timebase frequencies to the actual clock tree.
4. The intended operating temperature/voltage envelope is team-reviewed.

Ordered actions:
1. Identify whether the relevant timebase ultimately derives from HSI, HSE or another authorized source.
2. Bind the actual oscillator/crystal part and clock source configuration to the same firmware/board evidence packet.
3. Obtain minimum/maximum frequency bounds applicable over the reviewed operating conditions. For HSI, use the applicable datasheet range rather than the cover-page 1% summary. For HSE, bind the real oscillator/resonator datasheet and board implementation evidence.
4. Propagate that interval through the actual PLL/divider/timebase configuration from TC-03B11.
5. For every freshness, persistence, debounce, plausibility or timeout threshold, label whether it is a minimum-duration or maximum-age requirement.
6. Convert each requirement in the conservative direction and retain both ticks and real-time bounds.
7. Recheck wrap/half-range constraints after conversion.
8. Keep timestamp domains separate unless a reviewed synchronization/common-capture mapping exists.
9. Re-run this evidence if the clock source, trim, PLL, divider or operating envelope changes.

Stop conditions:
- oscillator source is unknown;
- a frequency bound is copied from a summary that does not apply to the operating envelope;
- HSE accuracy is guessed without the actual component evidence;
- runtime trimming or clock switching is possible but unmodeled;
- a minimum-duration and maximum-age requirement are treated as the same threshold problem;
- wrap/half-range is incompatible with the converted threshold;
- validation would require unapproved flashing or vehicle actuation.

No firmware flashing, CAN traffic, HIL, dyno, track testing, watchdog/shutdown change, Company Runtime mutation or vehicle actuation is authorized.
