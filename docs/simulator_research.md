# LapSim: simulator and driving-control research

Research checked 9 October 2026. This report compares publicly documented capabilities, not independently measured accuracy. Recommendations below are proposed interface and engineering work; they do not imply that those features have been added to LapSim.

## What other simulators teach us

A lap-time solver answers how quickly a model can complete a course under its assumptions. A driving simulator advances the vehicle's response to steering and pedal inputs; its path can depart from the intended line. A transient optimal-control solver can do the latter mathematics offline without providing a human driving interface. These are different useful modes, and a moving car animation alone does not distinguish them.

| Tool | Documented model and controls | Useful lesson for LapSim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **OpenLAP** | MATLAB point-mass lap-time and straight-line tools, with separate vehicle and track preparation. Track creation accepts shape or logged distance/speed/lateral-acceleration or yaw data, plus elevation, banking and grip factors. [Author's repository](https://github.com/mc12027/OpenLAP-Lap-Time-Simulator) | Keep course preparation, vehicle calibration and solving inspectable. Its vehicle source exposes torque curves, driveline ratios, tire friction/load sensitivity and brake hardware geometry. [OpenVEHICLE source](https://github.com/mc12027/OpenLAP-Lap-Time-Simulator/blob/master/OpenVEHICLE.m) |
| **OptimumLap** | Point-mass, quasi-steady lap estimation with a small vehicle input set, logged-track import and comparative plots. Its official description explicitly excludes weight transfer and transient effects. [OptimumG](https://students.optimumg.com/product/optimumlap/) | Offer clear sensitivity comparisons before adding more parameters. State exactly what a tire, mass, power or drag adjustment changes. |
| **Fastest-lap** | Transient optimal lap-time control with 3-DOF and 6-DOF vehicle models; track preprocessing yields a reference line, curvature and left/right limits. Uses numerical optimization rather than quasi-steady assumptions. [Author's repository](https://github.com/juanmanzanero/fastest-lap) | Distinguish a feasible controller replay from a globally optimal lap. | **IPG CarMaker** | Vehicle, powertrain, driver and road/traffic models for virtual test driving, including adaptive drivers and repeatable scenarios. [IPG product description](https://www.ipg-automotive.com/solutions/product-portfolio/carmaker) Human driver setups can use a wheel, pedals and shifter. [Driver-in-the-loop](https://www.ipg-automotive.com/solutions/product-portfolio/driver-in-the-loop) | Separate automated policy, human input and test scenario. | **VI-CarRealTime / VI-Driver** | Real-time vehicle dynamics and integration with controller and driving-simulator environments. [VI-grade](https://www.vi-grade.com/en/products/vi-carrealtime/) Its driver brochure describes configurable steering, throttle/brake and shifts, plus straight braking, braking in a turn and step/ramp/sine steer tests. [Official brochure](https://www.vi-grade.com/attachments/Oggetto/Database.Products/Allegati/Brochures/___d123af0b05394bac8770921e78fdf078_/VI-CarRealTime_2020.pdf) | Add a small maneuver library with expected physical checks, alongside complete laps. Each maneuver should expose its issued controls and vehicle response. |
| **iRacing** | Human steering/pedal input, controller calibration and car-specific setups. Brake Force Factor changes the response to pedal input; it is not hydraulic line pressure. [Official calibration guide](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000133335) A current Ferrari manual distinguishes slip-based TC, yaw-based TC and car-specific ABS strategies. [Ferrari 296 Challenge manual](https://s100.iracing.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Ferrari-296-Challenge.pdf) | Keep input-device response, brake hardware and stability controllers separate. | **Assetto Corsa** | Publisher-hosted controls distinguish throttle, brake, steering, traction-control cycling and adjustable brake balance for controller and wheel inputs. This source supports those controls; it does not establish a detailed hydraulic model or universal setup values. |

No car-specific pressures, tire pressures, suspension settings or assistance settings from another simulator should be copied into this vehicle without matching hardware and calibration. Public product descriptions establish feature scope, not real-car validation of LapSim.

## What this LapSim already implements

The following is based on the checked-out source. **Desktop** means exposed by the existing desktop workflow. **API** means implemented in Python but requires a suitable configuration/profile or additional interface. **Experiment** means the separate synthetic planar model.

| Input or mode | Units and behavior | Status and source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic vehicle setup | Mass kg, peak power kW, wheelbase m, rolling radius m, friction coefficient, drag area m², top speed km/h and torque request fraction 0–1. The Prius benchmark is an idealized power-equivalent model, not a calibrated hybrid/transaxle simulation. | Desktop: [VehicleSetup and benchmark](../src/lapsim/ui/presets.py) |
| Torque request | Motor-shaft torque N·m; nonnegative. Torque-fraction profiles are normalized requests, not a measured accelerator-pedal map. | API: [Controls](../src/lapsim/core/controls.py), [torque profiles](../src/lapsim/optimization/torque_profile.py) |
| Friction brakes | Separate front/rear hydraulic requests in psi, pressure deadband, axle torque gains and pressure cap. Linear hardware gains or a firmware force map convert requests to forces; grip and load transfer limit delivered forces. Default cap is **300 psi**, a model configuration value. | API: [Brakes](../src/vehicle_model/mech/brakes.py); the desktop controller generates pressures |
| Regenerative braking | Separate front/rear requested axle force N. Acceptance depends on driven axle, speed, motor/drivetrain limits, battery charge-power limits and SOC/voltage headroom. Drive and regeneration cannot be requested simultaneously from the same modeled motor. | API: [Controls](../src/lapsim/core/controls.py), [vehicle](../src/vehicle_model/vehicle.py), [drivetrain](../src/vehicle_model/powertrain/drivetrain.py), [battery models](../src/vehicle_model/electrical/battery.py) |

| Steering / control traces | Distance-indexed steering radians, torque, pressures and regen forces. Controls are held over each spatial cell; piecewise-linear profiles interpolate between distance knots. | Course choices and imports | Shipped 989 m fused GNSS/IMU course, analytic rounded loop, analytic FSAE-style practice course and validated course bundles. Imported widths retain their provenance; the AI planner currently uses an explicitly assumed corridor. | Desktop: [course catalog](../src/lapsim/ui/course_catalog.py), [course bundle schema](../src/lapsim/courses/course_bundle.py) |

Optional speed-periodic solving checks seam speed only. | Desktop/API: [event API](../src/lapsim/events/api.py), [racing line](../src/lapsim/optimization/racing_line.py), [simulation helpers](../src/lapsim/ui/simulation.py) |

Grip multipliers modify force caps, not a wet tire's complete behavior. | API/Experiment: [road grip mapping](../src/lapsim/optimization/road_grip_schedule.py), [PlanarEnvironment](../src/lapsim/dynamics/conditions.py) |
| Time-domain vehicle response | Actual x/y/heading, longitudinal/lateral velocity, yaw rate and four wheel speeds; per-wheel steering and drive/brake torques. Pose driver settings include lookahead, cruise speed, control gains, torque/steering bounds, initial offset and corridor margin. | Experiment: [planar dynamics](../src/lapsim/dynamics/planar.py), [PoseDriverSettings](../src/lapsim/optimization/pose_driver.py). Its duration is not an endurance-model lap time. |

The shipped fused course's plotted x/y and curvature are not one geometrically consistent path. Its map is useful evidence, but assumed AI clearance is not surveyed track clearance. Prefer a coherent synthetic/imported course when demonstrating actual pose tracking. Current models do not establish tire-temperature/wear effects, a complete ABS/TC controller, road banking/elevation dynamics or a calibrated real-car suspension/handling prediction.

## Brake controls and equations to expose

Show **request**, **applied value** and **limiting reason** separately. A driver may request more pressure or regeneration than the model can deliver.

For the linear brake model, with pressure `p`, deadband `p₀`, configured cap `p_max`, rolling radius `r` and axle gain `G` in N·m/psi:

```text
p_effective = max(min(p, p_max) - p₀, 0)
F_front_request = G_front · p_front_effective / r
F_rear_request  = G_rear  · p_rear_effective  / r
front requested force bias = F_front_request / (F_front_request + F_rear_request)
```

When the denominator is zero, the bias is undefined. Equal front/rear pressure does not mean equal braking force when axle gains differ. A future “front brake bias %” control should convert a requested total force into axle forces and then call `Brakes.axle_pressures_for_force_requests_psi`; it should not simply divide a single pressure by a percentage. Display friction-only and combined friction-plus-regen bias separately. [Brake conversion API](../src/vehicle_model/mech/brakes.py)

Keep pedal input as a separate normalized 0–1 signal, with an explicit calibrated pedal-to-pressure/torque map if human controls are added. Preserve psi in the existing brake API; optional bar or kPa display conversion must not change calibration. Use N for regen force, W/kW for battery power, 0–1 internally for SOC, m/s internally for speed and radians internally for steering. Do not label tire pressure and brake pressure with an ambiguous “pressure” field.

## Recommended driving styles and tweakable settings

iRacing's own setup guide describes trail braking as progressively releasing braking while turning and identifies throttle application point as a separate driver action. Its guidance is to compare incremental changes because setup adjustments have tradeoffs. [Official setup guide, pp. 19–21](https://ir-core-sites.iracing.com/members/pdfs/iRacing_Car_Setup_Guide_20100910.pdf) These concepts suggest **editable policies**, not a single “aggression” slider.

| Proposed policy | Concrete settings | Implementation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reference performance** | Current torque fraction, course, initial state, pressure cap and path speed controller. | Already available; describe it as the current controller, not a global optimum. |
| **Earlier braking / smooth release** | Brake onset station m, front/rear peak psi, build and release slopes psi/m, throttle pickup station m and torque ramp N·m/m. | Existing full-control profiles can represent traces. Automatic corner-aware preset generation and a profile editor are proposed work. A psi/s setting needs a speed-aware time-domain adapter. |
| **Trail braking** | Pressure release through turn-in, axle force bias and throttle pickup; overlay lateral/longitudinal grip use. | Prescribed-path models can evaluate force sharing. Claims about yaw rotation or oversteer need the separate transient pose model and calibrated tires. |
| **Lift and coast / energy saving** | Lift station or coast distance m, torque ceiling, regen-enabled policy, SOC threshold and charge-power cap. Compare time, net battery energy and final SOC. | Torque/control profiles and battery accounting exist. Automatic energy-target or time-budget optimization is proposed work. Match starting SOC and use only feasible completed runs. |
| **Conservative pace** | Target-speed fraction, braking preview/margin, smoother ramps and a larger corridor margin. | Pose cruise speed/lookahead/margin exist. A margin-aware main lap controller is proposed; reducing torque alone is not a complete cautious-driving model. |
| **Human driving** | Wheel steering, normalized pedals, calibrated input curves and optional assistance controllers. | Proposed. The existing Driver's View is solved-run playback; the autonomous pose experiment is not a human-input simulator. |

A profile switching between positive drive torque and regeneration needs an intervening zero-drive/zero-regen knot, so interpolation cannot request both simultaneously. Respect the force and path feasibility checks instead of silently clipping a bad policy into a completed lap.

Recommended interface groups are **Vehicle**, **Course and conditions**, **Driving policy**, **Battery and energy**, **Run settings** and **Results**. Each setting should expose its units, model/API name, default source and implementation status on expansion. Advanced vehicle inputs can surface existing chassis geometry and load distribution, roll stiffness/load-transfer split, tire friction/load tables, driveline ratio/efficiencies/inertias, motor curves and battery OCV/RC data. Require measured or explicitly assumed values rather than adding model-free camber, damper, tire-pressure, ABS or TC sliders. [Chassis](../src/vehicle_model/mech/chassis.py), [suspension](../src/vehicle_model/mech/suspension.py), [tire](../src/vehicle_model/mech/tire.py), [motor](../src/vehicle_model/powertrain/motor.py), [battery](../src/vehicle_model/electrical/battery.py)

## Validation before ranking a new style

Use straight acceleration/braking, constant-radius cornering, braking in a turn and step/ramp steering experiments before complete-lap comparisons. Check finite telemetry, nonnegative normal loads, shared tire-force capacity, force/energy balance, pressure and charge caps, SOC limits, path/corridor validity and replay agreement. Repeat a policy at a finer grid to check whether its advantage survives numerical sensitivity. Persist course revision/hash, vehicle state, complete controls, environment, solver settings and model identity with each result. An incomplete, invalid-road or infeasible run must not rank as a faster style.

Finally, correlate the intended vehicle with measured torque/power, hydraulic pressure-to-torque data, braking/acceleration traces, tire/load behavior and course geometry. Internal numerical consistency and passing software tests support the implementation; they do not substitute for real-car validation.
