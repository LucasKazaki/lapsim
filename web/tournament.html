Evidence, interpretation and data readiness

Every displayed trajectory comes from the powered four-wheel simulation. The fixed pure-pursuit driver and conditions are shared by candidates; genetic search changes torque-controller settings only. The default view renders meshes through Three.js WebGLRenderer. No learned driving policy, physical-car validation or hardware timing claim is made. Replay checks cover the supported objective declaration and finite ordered pose samples; they do not certify every imported channel or measured-car calibration.

Requested working target: hypothetical 2025-derived AWD. The available registry has 95 records and zero 2025 references; verified 2025 geometry, tire data and drivetrain mapping are missing. The demo is a separate synthetic independent-four-motor AWD fixture. DTI F-SiC is a researched supplier lead, not confirmed 2025 hardware.

Regen rejection is modeled for full pack, voltage, motor/speed, temperature and tire reserve. Friction blending is an assumed available actuator. The diagnostic without that fallback retains a failed run. Static temperature inputs provide an inhibit test, not thermal prediction.

PlotJuggler: numeric CSV with monotonic time_s and unit suffixes. Native and JavaScript CSV serialization/reparse checks pass. Automated browser file selection/download remains unverified after tool stalls; CSV data validation does not establish transfer success. No installed PlotJuggler round trip or live-stream integration is claimed.

The WebGL scene consumes the same logs as the legacy view. Body meshes are illustrative; height is not a new vehicle dynamics state. At most eight ghosts are visible, with the lowest observed proxy first; history retains whole-population results. Keyboard arrows orbit, plus/minus zoom, and Home resets. See the local acceptance receipt for the tested browser and viewport scope. The existing MP4 and still are the earlier projected data replay, not a recording of this WebGL scene.
